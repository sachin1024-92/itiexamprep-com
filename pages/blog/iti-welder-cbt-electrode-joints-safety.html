ITI Welder CBT: Electrode Current, Joint Types & Safety Checklist

Original study notes for Welder trainees — electrode and current selection logic, joint types that trip MCQs, and a shop-floor safety list you can save before AITT CBT.

24 Sep 2026 · Welder · ~9 min read

Welder theory CBT rewards clear cause-and-effect thinking: why this electrode, why this polarity, why this joint fails. These notes use general trade knowledge — not a copy of any NIMI book or coaching PDF. Confirm your batch syllabus on Bharat Skills.

Unofficial guide. Always verify exam pattern and dates on DGT / NCVT MIS / Skill India Digital. We do not host copyrighted NIMI PDFs.

1. Electrode & current selection (SMAW basics)

Most ITI Welder MCQs still lean on shielded metal arc welding (SMAW / stick). Memorise the ideas, then the codes your instructor uses:

  • Diameter vs current: thicker electrodes generally need higher current. Too low → sticky arc, poor fusion; too high → undercut, burn-through on thin plate, excess spatter.
  • Rule-of-thumb range: many shops start near ~30–40 A per mm of electrode diameter (example: 3.15 mm ≈ 90–125 A), then fine-tune for position and plate thickness. Always follow the electrode maker’s chart in real work.
  • Polarity: DCEP (electrode positive / reverse) often gives deeper penetration for many covered electrodes; DCEN (electrode negative / straight) can run cooler on the workpiece for some jobs. AC electrodes are designed for AC machines — don’t mix ideas blindly.
  • Coating role: flux produces slag + shielding gas, stabilises the arc, and shapes bead appearance. Damp electrodes → porosity risk; bake/store as taught for low-hydrogen types.
  • Coding awareness: AWS-style E6013 / E7018-type labels encode tensile class and usability (position / current type). Know what your syllabus expects; don’t invent numbers in the exam.

Exam tip: if an option claims “any current works for any diameter,” reject it. Current must match electrode size, position, and joint thickness.

2. Joint types students confuse on MCQs

Name the joint from how the plates meet — drawings and word problems love mixing these:

  • Butt joint: edges face each other in the same plane. Edge prep (square, V, U, double-V) depends on thickness and access.
  • Lap joint: plates overlap; fillet welds often used on the overlap edges. Easy fit-up, but watch undercut and incomplete fusion.
  • T-joint: one plate meets another at ~90°. Common fillet welds; root access and clean mill scale matter.
  • Corner joint: edges meet at a corner (box sections, frames). Open vs closed corner changes heat input needs.
  • Edge joint: edges of parallel plates joined — thinner work, lower load capacity; don’t confuse with butt.
  • Positions (1G–4G / 1F–4F idea): flat is easiest; vertical and overhead need tighter current control and shorter arcs. Match position letters to your syllabus chart.

Trap: a “fillet” is a weld type, not a joint name. You can put a fillet on a T, lap, or corner joint.

3. Quick defect → cause map (CBT favourites)

  • Porosity: trapped gas — moisture, paint/oil, poor shielding, damp electrodes.
  • Undercut: groove melted into base metal at the toe — often excess current / travel speed / wrong angle.
  • Slag inclusion: slag trapped in weld — poor cleaning between passes or wrong manipulation.
  • Lack of fusion / incomplete penetration: heat or prep insufficient — low current, wide gap, dirty joint, wrong technique.
  • Crack: severe — restraint, wrong consumable, hydrogen (low-H practice), poor cooling control. Flag and report; don’t “cover up.”

4. Shop-floor welding safety checklist (save this)

  • Helmet with correct shade filter; never watch the arc with bare eyes (arc eye risk)
  • Leather / flame-resistant gloves, apron, closed footwear — no synthetic melt risk near spatter
  • Ventilation or fume extraction; extra caution on coatings (e.g. galvanized zinc fumes)
  • Dry floor, clear fire triangle; keep extinguisher nearby; fire watch after hot work
  • Cylinders upright, secured; identify gas by approved markings — never guess colour alone
  • Cables, holders, and earth clamp in good condition; isolate power before changing leads
  • Screens protect nearby workers; remove flammables in the zone before striking an arc

5. Short FAQ

Q. Is higher current always better penetration?
A. Up to a point — excess current causes undercut, burn-through, and spatter. Match electrode diameter and plate thickness.

Q. Do I need to memorise every electrode code?
A. Learn the coding system and the common electrodes in your syllabus; confirm the list on Bharat Skills for your trade code.

Q. Why do MCQs ask about slag cleaning?
A. Slag left between passes causes inclusions and hides defects — cleaning is both quality and safety practice.

6. Exemplar MCQs (original)

Practice / exemplar questions — not from any leaked paper. Written for learning only.

Q1. Compared with a 2.5 mm covered electrode, a 4 mm electrode of the same type generally needs:
A. Much lower current
B. Higher current in the recommended range
C. Zero current
D. Only AC if the small one used DC
Answer: B — Larger diameter electrodes typically require higher welding current.
Q2. Two plates placed edge-to-edge in the same plane form a:
A. Lap joint
B. Butt joint
C. Edge joint of overlapping sheets only
D. T-joint
Answer: B — Butt joint: edges face each other in one plane.
Q3. A groove melted into the base metal beside the weld toe is called:
A. Porosity
B. Undercut
C. Slag taste test
D. Perfect reinforcement
Answer: B — Undercut weakens the joint if excessive; often linked to high current or poor technique.
Q4. Before striking an arc near painted or oily plate, the safest study answer is:
A. Ignore contamination — it always burns clean
B. Clean / remove contaminants and ensure ventilation as required
C. Use sunglasses only
D. Increase current to maximum always
Answer: B — Contamination drives porosity and fumes; clean and ventilate.

  • Day 1–2: SMAW electrode diameter, current ranges, polarity ideas
  • Day 3: Joint types + weld types (fillet vs groove) vocabulary
  • Day 4: Defects and causes (porosity, undercut, slag, LOF)
  • Day 5: Positions + distortion control basics
  • Day 6: Safety + cylinders + Employability Skills (don’t skip ES)
