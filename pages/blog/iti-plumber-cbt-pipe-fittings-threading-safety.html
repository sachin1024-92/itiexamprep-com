ITI Plumber CBT: Pipe Fittings, Threading & Plumbing Safety Checklist

A practical, original revision map for Plumber trainees — pipe materials & uses, fittings CBT traps, threading & joints, measurement & slope habits, and plumbing workshop/site safety for AITT CBT.

3 Oct 2026 · Plumber · ~9 min read

Plumber theory CBT rewards clear purpose thinking. Explain why a union is used for maintenance joints, or why drainage lines need a fall. Original study advice — not a NIMI copy. Confirm syllabus on Bharat Skills.

Unofficial guide. Always verify exam pattern and dates on DGT / NCVT MIS / Skill India Digital. We do not host copyrighted NIMI PDFs.

1. Pipe materials & uses flash cards (memorise these)

  • GI pipe: galvanised steel for many cold-water uses; threaded joints classic — know the idea, not brand coating tables.
  • CI pipe: heavy piping for soil/waste/drainage ideas — not “always for drinking water.”
  • PVC / CPVC: plastics for cold (PVC) and hot/cold (CPVC) as taught; solvent joints ≠ metal threading.
  • Copper: soldering/brazing services as taught; soft vs hard for bend vs rigid runs.
  • PPR: heat fusion joints — not PVC solvent cement. CBT loves that swap trap.
  • Material habit: match fluid, temperature, pressure, indoor/outdoor — not “any pipe anywhere.”

Exam tip: reject “PVC cement for every PPR and GI threaded line.” Match joint method to material.

2. Common fittings CBT traps (purpose thinking)

Learn the job of each fitting — papers love swapping names:

  • Elbow: changes direction — not a tee / branch take-off.
  • Tee: creates a branch (three openings) — not “just another elbow.”
  • Reducer: size change — not a valve or clean-out.
  • Union: openable joint for maintenance — vs permanent coupling.
  • Coupling / socket: joins same-size pipes in-line — not a union.
  • Nipple: short threaded pipe bridge — not a valve or elbow.
  • Valves: isolation / throttle / check purpose — not brand handle colours.

Trap: “A union permanently welds pipes so they never open” is wrong.

3. Threading, joints & tools favourites

  • Die & stock: cut external threads — purpose is mating threads, not decoration.
  • Pipe wrench / vice: grip and support; don’t crush thin plastic with heavy jaws.
  • Thread seal: helps seal metal threads as taught — does not replace correct threads.
  • Solvent weld: PVC/CPVC fusion of sockets — ≠ solder ≠ PPR fusion.
  • Soldering / brazing: copper joints with fire-safety habits as taught.
  • Joint habit: pick thread / solvent / fusion / solder by material — don’t swap blindly.

Exam tip: solvent cement does not thread GI like a die.

4. Measurement, slope & layout habits

  • Fall / slope: drainage needs controlled fall — zero fall is a trap.
  • Marking: measure twice; allow fitting engagement depths.
  • Tape habits: readable tape, correct start, account for fittings.
  • Layout: straight runs, sensible supports, align before final tighten.
  • Levels: spirit level / plumb for stacks and falls — not eyeballing only.
  • Access: plan valves/clean-outs — don’t bury a union forever.

Exam tip: “Always lay waste pipes with zero fall” is wrong.

5. Plumbing workshop / site safety checklist (high-yield)

  • PPE: eye protection for cutting/chipping, gloves, footwear, dust/mask as taught
  • Tool care: sharp cutters, correct wrenches, secure vices
  • Hot work & solvents: fire watch / clear flammables; ventilate cements — no smoking
  • Pressure & isolation: isolate/depressurise before opening joints; prove zero pressure
  • Site hazards: trenches, wet floors, sharp ends, overhead — barricade/housekeeping
  • Hygiene: keep potable materials clean; wash after soil/waste work

6. Three exemplar MCQs (original)

Practice / exemplar questions — not from any leaked paper. Written for learning only.

Q1. Which statement best describes the main purpose of a pipe union?
A. It only measures pipe diameter like a micrometer
B. It joins pipes so the joint can be opened later for maintenance without cutting the whole run
C. It permanently stores solvent cement inside the pipe
D. It replaces the need for any elbow or tee on every installation
Answer: B — Union = serviceable joint that can be opened.
Q2. A trainee says PVC solvent cement works for GI threads and PPR fusion alike. Which correction is best?
A. Yes — one cement works for GI, PVC, and PPR the same way
B. Match the joint to the material: GI threads; PVC/CPVC solvent; PPR heat fusion
C. None of these materials is ever joined in plumbing
D. Solvent cement only cools the pipe wrench jaws
Answer: B — Joint method follows material.
Q3. Which pair correctly matches a Plumber workshop/site safety habit?
A. Open joints without isolating, assuming every valve is at zero pressure
B. Eye protection, ventilate solvents, hot-work care, isolate before opening, keep potable lines clean
C. Smoke freely while cementing PVC indoors for better curing
D. Always lay waste pipes with zero fall so water stands still
Answer: B — PPE, vented solvents, isolate before opening, protect potable water.

7. One-week Plumber theory sprint

  • Day 1–2: Pipe materials — GI, CI, PVC/CPVC, copper, PPR; match to service
  • Day 3: Fittings — elbow, tee, reducer, union, coupling, nipple, valve purpose
  • Day 4: Threading & joints — die/stock, wrenches, seal, solvent, solder/fusion
  • Day 5: Measurement & layout — fall/slope, marking, tape, alignment, levels
  • Day 6: Workshop/site safety + Employability Skills
  • Day 7: Timed Plumber mini-mock + error-notebook repair only
