ITI Electrician CBT: Earthing, MCB/RCCB & Safety Checklist

Original study notes for Electrician trainees — why we earth, how protective devices differ, and a shop-floor electrical safety list you can save before AITT CBT.

24 Sep 2026 · Electrician · ~9 min read

Electrician theory CBT rewards clear cause-and-effect thinking: why this earth electrode, why this breaker curve, why isolation before work. These notes use general trade knowledge — not a copy of any NIMI book or coaching PDF. Confirm your batch syllabus on Bharat Skills.

Unofficial guide. Always verify exam pattern and dates on DGT / NCVT MIS / Skill India Digital. We do not host copyrighted NIMI PDFs.

1. Earthing — purpose students must not mix up

Earthing (grounding) is not “extra wire decoration.” Learn the jobs separately:

  • Protective earthing: connects exposed conductive parts (motor frame, DB body, appliance casing) to earth so a fault current can trip the protective device and keep touch voltage low.
  • System / neutral earthing: connects the supply neutral (or star point) to earth at the source side — different role from bonding every metal body in the installation.
  • Low earth resistance idea: a low-resistance path helps fault current rise enough for the MCB/fuse to clear. Dry sandy soil, loose clamps, and corroded electrodes raise resistance — MCQs love this.
  • Pipe / plate / rod electrodes (idea level): know that electrode type, depth, moisture, and salt/charcoal backfill (where taught) affect resistance. Follow your syllabus method; don’t invent numbers in the exam.
  • Earth wire continuity: broken CPC (circuit protective conductor) = metal body may stay live under fault with no reliable trip path. Continuity tests matter in practical and theory wording.

Exam tip: if an option says earthing is only “to save electricity bill,” reject it. Earthing is about safety and fault clearing, not energy saving.

2. MCB, fuse, RCCB/ELCB — selection logic

Name the device by what it senses, not by colour of the handle:

  • Fuse: melts on sustained overcurrent. Simple, cheap, one-time. Rating must match cable and load — never “upgrade” blindly with a thicker fuse wire.
  • MCB (miniature circuit breaker): thermal + magnetic trip for overload and short-circuit. Reusable after fault cleared. Curve types (B/C/D idea) change instantaneous trip sensitivity — motors may need a less sensitive curve than lighting circuits (as per syllabus).
  • RCCB / residual current device: trips on imbalance between live and neutral (earth leakage / residual current) — protects against shock from leakage to earth. It does not replace overcurrent protection.
  • ELCB wording: older voltage-operated ELCB ideas appear in some texts; modern residual-current devices sense current imbalance. Read the question carefully for which principle it asks.
  • MCB + RCCB (or RCBO) together: overload/short-circuit and leakage protection are different layers. Knowing “which device for which fault” is a high-yield CBT skill.
  • Incomer vs outgoing: main switch / isolator for isolation; branch MCBs for individual circuits. Isolation before maintenance is a safety answer, not optional etiquette.

Trap: an RCCB will not reliably clear a short between live and neutral if there is no residual to earth. Overcurrent devices handle that path.

3. Cable, colour & wiring practice (CBT favourites)

  • Current-carrying capacity: thicker conductor → lower resistance → can carry more current for a given temperature rise. Undersized cable + high load → heating and fire risk.
  • Voltage drop idea: long runs drop voltage; motors may stall or run hot. Identify the concept even if exact tables are not memorised.
  • Insulation & mechanical protection: damaged insulation = shock/fire. Use correct conduit/trunking practices taught in your workshop.
  • Colour / identification: follow the colour code your syllabus and local practice teach for phase, neutral, and earth — never assume from memory of a different country code.
  • Single-phase vs three-phase basics: know where 230 V and 400 V class supplies appear in Indian installations at idea level; match loads and protection accordingly.

4. Shop-floor electrical safety checklist (save this)

  • Isolate and verify dead (test before touch) — never trust a switch position alone
  • Use insulated tools and PPE rated for the work; dry footwear and standing area
  • Lockout / tagout when available; warn others before re-energising
  • No jewellery or loose metal near live work; keep one hand free practice where taught
  • Correct extinguisher awareness for electrical fires (do not use water on live equipment)
  • Check earth continuity and polarity on new or repaired circuits before handover
  • Report damaged cables, open DBs, and missing covers instead of “making do”

5. Short FAQ

Q. Does a bigger MCB always mean safer?
A. No — oversized protection may not trip before the cable overheats. Match breaker rating to cable and load design.

Q. Is earthing the same as neutral?
A. Related but not identical jobs. Neutral carries return current in normal operation; protective earth is for fault safety. Mixing them incorrectly is dangerous and often wrong in MCQs.

Q. Why test an RCCB with the test button?
A. The test button simulates residual current so you know the mechanism still works — a silent failed device gives false confidence.

6. Exemplar MCQs (original)

Practice / exemplar questions — not from any leaked paper. Written for learning only.

Q1. The main safety purpose of connecting an appliance metal body to earth is to:
A. Reduce the electricity bill
B. Provide a low-impedance fault path so protection can clear and limit shock risk
C. Increase normal load current
D. Replace the need for insulation entirely
Answer: B — Protective earthing helps clear faults and reduce touch hazard.
Q2. An RCCB is primarily designed to detect:
A. Only high power-factor loads
B. Residual / leakage current (imbalance between live and neutral)
C. Cable colour only
D. Transformer oil temperature only
Answer: B — RCCB senses residual current imbalance.
Q3. Compared with a correctly rated fuse, repeatedly replacing a blown fuse with a much thicker wire is:
A. Best practice for motors
B. Dangerous — protection may not clear before cable damage / fire risk
C. Required by every RCCB
D. A method to earth the neutral
Answer: B — Oversizing the fusible element defeats protection.
Q4. Before opening a distribution board for maintenance, the safest study answer is:
A. Touch busbars quickly to “check” voltage
B. Isolate supply, verify dead with a suitable tester, then proceed with PPE
C. Only switch off one outgoing lamp circuit
D. Remove the earth wire first always
Answer: B — Isolate, verify dead, use PPE.
