ITI CBT 30-Day Study Plan & 12 Common Mistakes to Avoid

A practical, original plan for CTS trainees who want structured revision before AITT CBT — without relying on leaked papers.

24 Sep 2026 · Study Strategy · ~10 min read

Most ITI trainees do not fail for lack of intelligence — they lose marks to uneven revision, ignored Employability Skills, and exam-day panic. This guide gives a 30-day structure you can adapt to Electrician, Fitter, COPA, Welder, Diesel, Electronics, or MMV, plus twelve mistakes we see repeatedly in coaching conversations.

This article is original study advice. It is not affiliated with DGT/NCVT. Confirm dates and paper pattern on official portals before the exam.

Who this plan fits

  • You have roughly one month before CBT (or a mock cycle you treat as a deadline).
  • You already attended practical classes — this plan focuses on theory CBT readiness.
  • You can study 2–3 focused hours on weekdays and a longer block on weekends.

The 30-day blueprint

Days 1–3 — Map the battlefield

Open your trade syllabus on Bharat Skills. Write three lists: (A) strong topics, (B) shaky topics, (C) never studied. Skim the publicly known CBT shape: about 2 hours, MCQs, typically 2 marks each, no negative marking, with Employability Skills often around 25 questions for many papers. Your exact session may differ — verify officially.

Days 4–14 — Build coverage

Each weekday: 75 minutes Trade Theory from list B/C + 25 minutes Employability Skills + 20 minutes WCS or Drawing (engineering) or extra Theory (non-engineering). End with 10 self-made or practice MCQs. Keep an error notebook: wrong concept, correct idea, one example.

Days 15–22 — Pressure practice

Alternate days: timed mini-mocks (30–40 questions, 40–50 minutes) and deep repair of error-notebook topics. Once in this window, attempt a near-full mock under exam timing. Review every wrong answer the same day — skipping review wastes the mock.

Days 23–27 — High-yield polish

Stop opening brand-new chapters unless they are must-know safety or tools topics. Drill formulas, definitions, PPE, earthing/safety (for electrical trades), fits/least count (fitter), OS/network basics (COPA), welding defects (welder), and ES soft-skill definitions.

Days 28–30 — Taper

Light revision only. Sleep well. Print admit card and check centre. Pack ID and stationery. Mentally walk through: “I will attempt known questions first, mark doubtful ones, return later.”

12 common mistakes that cost CBT marks

  1. Ignoring Employability Skills until the last week — ES is often a large, relatively learnable block.
  2. Only reading notes, never practising MCQs under time — CBT is a speed + accuracy skill.
  3. Skipping Workshop Calculation because “only few questions” — easy marks if formulas are ready.
  4. Memorising without understanding safety logic — many questions twist PPE / earthing / fire types.
  5. Studying from random WhatsApp PDFs of unknown origin — prefer syllabus + your own notes + official portals.
  6. Not keeping an error log — you re-fail the same concept in the real exam.
  7. Changing answers without reason in the last minutes — usually lowers score.
  8. Spending too long on one hard question early — protect easy marks first.
  9. Neglecting sleep before CBT — fatigue kills reading accuracy on long MCQ stems.
  10. Assuming negative marking when your session has none (or the reverse) — read instructions on screen.
  11. Ignoring Hindi/English bilingual terms — know common trade words in both; this site’s language toggle helps UI practice.
  12. Trusting unofficial “leak” claims — risky, unethical, and often wrong; practise with original exemplar questions instead.

Simple weekly scoreboard

Track four numbers each Sunday: (1) chapters revised, (2) timed MCQs attempted, (3) ES modules finished, (4) sleep nights ≥7 hours. If any number is zero, fix that before adding new content.
