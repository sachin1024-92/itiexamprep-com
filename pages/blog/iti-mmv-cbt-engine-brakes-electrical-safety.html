ITI MMV CBT: Engine Systems, Brakes, Auto Electrical & Workshop Safety Checklist

A practical, original revision map for Mechanic Motor Vehicle (MMV) trainees — powertrain & engine ideas, brakes & steering CBT traps, auto electrical favourites, and workshop/road safety for AITT CBT.

26 Sep 2026 · Mechanic Motor Vehicle · ~9 min read

MMV theory CBT rewards clear system-purpose thinking more than memorising brand catalogues. If you can explain why a clutch disconnects drive, or why hydraulic brakes multiply pedal force, twisted options become easier. This checklist is original study advice — not a copy of any NIMI book or coaching PDF. Confirm your batch syllabus on Bharat Skills.

Unofficial guide. Always verify exam pattern and dates on DGT / NCVT MIS / Skill India Digital. We do not host copyrighted NIMI PDFs.

1. Powertrain / engine flash cards (memorise these)

  • 4-stroke idea: intake → compression → power → exhaust across four piston strokes (two crank revolutions). Most light-vehicle engines trainees meet are four-stroke.
  • Petrol vs diesel combustion cue: petrol engines typically use spark ignition; diesel engines use compression ignition — hot compressed air ignites injected fuel. Mixing “diesel always needs spark plugs” is a classic distractor.
  • Cooling purpose: removes excess combustion heat so parts stay within a safe temperature range. Overheating risks warping, oil breakdown, and seizure — know the job of the cooling circuit, not obscure thermostat part numbers.
  • Lubrication purpose: reduces friction and wear, helps carry heat and contaminants, and cushions moving parts. Cooling ≠ lubrication — coolant and oil belong in different systems.
  • Clutch purpose: temporarily disconnects (and smoothly reconnects) engine drive from the gearbox so you can start from rest, change gears, and avoid driveline shock. It is not a brake and not a permanent “power cut” device.
  • Transmission idea: matches engine speed/torque to road demand via gear ratios. Know the purpose (multiply torque / allow efficient cruising); do not invent brand-specific ratio tables for MCQs.

Exam tip: if an option says “the clutch’s only job is to stop the wheels like a service brake,” reject it. Clutch = drive connect/disconnect; brakes = slow/stop the vehicle.

2. Brakes & steering CBT traps

Learn the idea behind each system — papers love mixing hydraulic, ABS, and geometry vocabulary:

  • Hydraulic brake idea: pedal force is transmitted through brake fluid in a closed system and multiplied at the wheel cylinders/calipers (Pascal’s law idea). Air in the lines can make the pedal spongy because air compresses; fluid (ideally) does not.
  • Service brake vs parking brake: service brakes slow/stop while driving; parking/hand brake holds the vehicle when stationary. Mixing their primary jobs is a common wrong option pattern.
  • ABS purpose (high level): Anti-lock Braking System helps prevent wheel lock-up under hard braking so the driver can keep steering control and reduce skid risk on many surfaces. It does not “make braking distance always shorter on every road” as a blanket claim — know the control idea.
  • Steering geometry — toe: whether front wheels point slightly inward or outward when viewed from above. Incorrect toe often shows as uneven tyre wear or wander — learn the concept name, not workshop alignment machine menus.
  • Camber idea: inward/outward tilt of the wheel when viewed from the front. Extreme incorrect camber can accelerate tyre edge wear. Trainees often confuse camber with toe — keep the viewing plane clear (front view vs top view).
  • Caster idea (general): related to steering self-centring / straight-line stability feeling. CBT usually tests recognition of the term’s role, not obscure degree tables from a specific OEM.

Trap: “ABS means you never need to leave a safe following distance” is wrong. ABS aids control under hard braking; safe driving habits still matter.

3. Auto electrical MCQ favourites

  • Battery care idea: keep terminals clean and tight; watch electrolyte level on serviceable batteries as taught; avoid deep discharge habits; never short across terminals with tools. Battery stores electrical energy for starting and loads.
  • Fuse vs relay idea: a fuse is a sacrificial over-current protector that opens when current is too high; a relay is an electrically controlled switch that lets a small control current switch a larger load circuit. Mixing “fuse switches high current on command” is a distractor.
  • Charging system purpose: while the engine runs, the alternator/charging circuit replenishes the battery and supplies electrical loads. A healthy battery can still go flat if charging has failed over a drive.
  • Starter circuit idea: starter motor briefly cranks the engine to start. High current flows on a dedicated path; poor connections, a flat battery, or a faulty starter solenoid/relay idea can cause “click / no crank” symptoms — learn roles, not invented ohm values.
  • Starter ≠ charging: starter works at start; charging works during run. Papers love swapping their jobs in options.
  • Lighting / earth return habit: many vehicle circuits need a sound earth/return path. Intermittent lamps can be bulb, fuse, switch, wiring, or earth — CBT rewards systematic thinking over guessing one part every time.

Exam tip: if an option says “a fuse is designed to repeatedly switch the starter motor on and off like a relay,” reject it. Fuse = protection; relay = controlled switching.

4. Workshop / road-safety checklist (high-yield)

  • Jack stands / axle stands under a raised vehicle — never rely on a jack alone for work underneath
  • Fuel and vapour caution: no smoking or open flames near petrol/diesel spills and rags; wipe/contain spills promptly; ventilate closed bays
  • Battery acid & PPE: eye/skin protection; avoid shorting terminals; follow instructor rules for boost/charge and disconnect order
  • Lifting & handling: use correct equipment and team lifts for heavy assemblies; keep back straight / load close; never stand under an unsupported load
  • Hot surfaces & rotating parts: exhaust, coolant, belts, and fans stay hazardous after shutdown or while running — keep clear, use guards, wait to cool
  • Road / test-drive habits: secure tools, check brakes before road test, use a helper/spotter in the yard, obey shop and traffic rules

5. Three exemplar MCQs (original)

Practice / exemplar questions — not from any leaked paper. Written for learning only.

Q1. Which statement best describes the main purpose of a vehicle clutch?
A. It permanently locks the wheels so the vehicle cannot roll
B. It temporarily disconnects and smoothly reconnects engine drive from the gearbox for starting and gear changes
C. It cools the radiator by pumping coolant when the pedal is pressed
D. It replaces the need for a battery in the charging system
Answer: B — Clutch = controlled drive connect/disconnect between engine and gearbox.
Q2. A spongy brake pedal after work on hydraulic brakes most usefully suggests which idea first?
A. Air may be in the brake lines — air compresses and can make the pedal feel soft; bleeding restores a solid fluid column
B. The alternator always causes spongy pedals
C. Toe-in settings control brake-fluid pressure directly
D. A fuse is the only possible cause of a soft pedal
Answer: A — Hydraulic brakes rely on an incompressible fluid column; air is a classic spongy-pedal idea.
Q3. Which pair correctly contrasts a fuse and a relay in auto electrical systems?
A. Both are only used to store charge like a battery
B. A fuse protects against excess current by opening; a relay uses a small control current to switch a larger load circuit
C. A fuse multiplies pedal force in the brake circuit; a relay cools the engine oil
D. Relays always melt before fuses and never switch circuits
Answer: B — Fuse = over-current protection; relay = electrically controlled switching.

6. One-week MMV theory sprint

  • Day 1–2: Engine / powertrain — 4-stroke idea, petrol vs diesel ignition cue, cooling vs lubrication, clutch & transmission purpose
  • Day 3: Brakes — hydraulic idea, air-in-line symptom, service vs parking brake, ABS purpose at high level
  • Day 4: Steering geometry concepts — toe, camber, caster (recognition, not OEM degree tables)
  • Day 5: Auto electrical — battery care, fuse vs relay, charging vs starter circuit ideas
  • Day 6: Workshop & road safety (stands, fuel/vapour, battery/PPE, lifting, hot/rotating parts) + Employability Skills
  • Day 7: Timed MMV mini-mock / trade MCQs + error-notebook repair only
