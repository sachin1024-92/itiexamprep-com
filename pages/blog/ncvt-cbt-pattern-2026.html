NCVT CBT Pattern 2026: Marks, Duration & What to Expect

Understand the All India Trade Test computer-based exam so your revision matches the real paper.

Published 24 Sep 2026 · ~9 min read · ITI Exam Prep

Every year, ITI trainees search for the latest AITT CBT pattern — and rumours spread faster than official circulars. This guide summarises the widely followed CTS CBT structure for 2026 so you can plan calmly. Always double-check notifications on dgt.gov.in and ncvtmis.gov.in for your exact session.

CBT in one glance

The theory All India Trade Test is conducted as a Computer Based Test. You sit at a terminal, read MCQs on screen, and select answers within a fixed duration. Typical parameters trainees prepare for:

  • Duration: 2 hours
  • Question type: Multiple choice
  • Marking: 2 marks per question
  • Negative marking: None
  • Indicative pass: Around 33%

No negative marking is a gift — but only if you manage time and eliminate options intelligently instead of random-clicking everything in the last 30 seconds.

Engineering trades: 75 questions, 150 marks

For popular engineering trades such as Electrician, Fitter, Electronics Mechanic, Mechanic Diesel, MMV, and many Welder pathways, the CBT mix is usually:

  • Trade Theory — 38 questions (76 marks)
  • Workshop Calculation & Science — 6 questions (12 marks)
  • Engineering Drawing — 6 questions (12 marks)
  • Employability Skills — 25 questions (50 marks)

That means Trade Theory plus Employability Skills alone account for 126 of 150 marks. If your study plan ignores ES, you are leaving a huge scoring zone untouched.

Non-engineering trades (example: COPA)

Non-engineering trades commonly emphasise Trade Theory and Employability Skills — often 50 + 25 questions. Confirm the exact blueprint for your trade code and year, because CTS documents can refine subject lists even when the overall CBT style stays similar.

How this should change your revision

  1. Build a marks map — print or note the subject split and assign weekly hours proportional to marks.
  2. Protect ES time — 25 questions is not “extra”; it is a full section.
  3. Keep WCS/Drawing warm — twelve marks each can decide borderline pass/fail when theory is average.
  4. Practise on screen — use free mocks so reading questions digitally feels normal.

What CBT does not replace

Practical skills, formative assessments, and institute-level requirements still matter. Passing CBT theory is necessary but not the whole certificate journey. Stay regular at your ITI workshop and keep records updated on NCVT MIS / Skill India Digital as instructed by your institute.

Official links worth bookmarking

  • Bharat Skills — syllabi and learning resources
  • DGT — policy and exam-related circulars
  • NCVT MIS / Skill India Digital — trainee profile, hall ticket instructions as applicable

Time management inside the CBT lab

Two hours sounds generous until the first calculation question slows you down. A practical split many toppers use:

  • 0–10 minutes: Settle in, read instructions, attempt a few easy ES or definition questions to build rhythm.
  • 10–70 minutes: Trade Theory bulk — skip anything that needs heavy calculation and flag it.
  • 70–100 minutes: WCS, Drawing, and flagged theory.
  • Last 20 minutes: Review flagged items and ensure every question has an answer selected.

Because the interface may differ slightly across exam vendors, practise scrolling, flagging, and reviewing in any CBT demo your ITI provides. Our on-site mocks train the decision habit even if the UI is simpler.

Language of the paper

Questions may appear in English, Hindi, or bilingual formats depending on the session. Technical nouns (megger, transformer, RCCB, clutch, electrode) often stay close to English even in Hindi stems. Make a two-column glossary for your trade: Hindi shop language ↔ exam English. That single sheet removes panic when a familiar concept uses an unfamiliar word.

Myths to ignore in 2026

  • “Only Trade Theory matters.” ES alone is 50 marks in the common blueprint.
  • “Someone will leak the paper.” That path is illegal and usually fake — and it steals study time.
  • “Pass is automatic if attendance is good.” Formative and practical pieces matter, but CBT theory still needs deliberate prep.
  • “Mock tests must be 75 questions or useless.” Short, explained quizzes improve accuracy faster at the start; lengthen mocks in the final fortnight.

After the exam

Note topics that felt weak while memory is fresh. If you have another attempt window or a complementary assessment, those notes become your next plan. Watch only official channels for result timelines — random “ITI result 2026” blogs often run ads or phishing pages.

Aligning with Bharat Skills and your ITI timetable

The best CBT plan is the one that respects your institute calendar. If practical exams or formative assessments fall in the same month as CBT, protect mornings for theory MCQs and afternoons for workshop skill polish. Use Bharat Skills modules as a second explanation when a NIMI chapter feels dense — video plus text improves recall for many trainees.

Also track circulars that change reporting times, ID rules, or subject codes. Pattern numbers in this article are a study map, not a substitute for the notice that arrives for your batch. When in doubt, ask your training officer and verify on DGT or NCVT MIS rather than WhatsApp forwards.

Finally, treat health as part of exam prep: hydration, short walks between study blocks, and limited late-night scrolling. A clear head reads MCQ options more carefully than an exhausted one — and careful reading is half the battle in CBT.
