ITI Machinist CBT: Milling Operations, Cutters & Workshop Safety Checklist

A practical, original revision map for Machinist trainees — milling machine basics, common milling operation CBT traps, cutters & tooling favourites, workholding & measuring habits, and workshop safety for AITT CBT.

3 Oct 2026 · Machinist · ~9 min read

Machinist theory CBT rewards clear purpose thinking. Explain why a face mill flatters a surface, or why a dividing head spaces holes around a circle. Original study advice — not a NIMI copy. Confirm syllabus on Bharat Skills.

Unofficial guide. Always verify exam pattern and dates on DGT / NCVT MIS / Skill India Digital. We do not host copyrighted NIMI PDFs.

1. Milling machine basics flash cards (memorise these)

  • Column: main vertical body that supports the knee and spindle drive — think rigidity backbone, not “just decoration.”
  • Knee: vertical slide that raises/lowers the table assembly — height control idea.
  • Table: work surface that moves longitudinally (and often cross via saddle) — where the job sits and feeds.
  • Spindle: rotating drive that holds arbor/cutter or vertical spindle tooling — power to the cutter.
  • Arbor: shaft that mounts cutters (esp. horizontal) between spindle and overarm support — not a measuring tool.
  • Horizontal vs vertical: horizontal spindle + arbor suits slab/side milling ideas; vertical spindle suits face/end milling and many setups as taught — purpose, not brand badge.

Exam tip: reject “the arbor measures workpiece diameter like a micrometer.” Arbor mounts cutters; micrometers measure.

2. Common milling operation CBT traps (purpose thinking)

Learn the job of each operation — papers love swapping names:

  • Face milling: flattens a face with the cutter’s end/face action — not the same as knurling a grip pattern.
  • Peripheral / slab milling: removes metal with cutter teeth on the periphery — broad flats/strips as taught.
  • End milling: slots, pockets, profiles with an end mill — not “only for facing large plates.”
  • Slotting: cuts a groove/keyway-style slot — purpose is a controlled slot, not random drilling.
  • Indexing / dividing: spaces features around a circle (holes, teeth ideas) using dividing head — not “guess-mark with chalk only.”
  • Operation habit: pick cutter + setup for the surface you need — face vs periphery vs slot — don’t swap blindly.

Trap: “Indexing only means painting numbers on the table dial” is wrong — dividing spaces angular features.

3. Cutters & tooling favourites

  • Plain / slab cutter: cylindrical periphery teeth for slab milling — purpose = peripheral cut, not a drill bit.
  • Face mill: large cutter for facing flats — inserts/teeth on the face as taught.
  • End mill: shank cutter for slots, pockets, profiles — end + side cutting ideas.
  • Side-and-face cutter: teeth on side and periphery for slots/shoulders — not a form cutter.
  • Form cutters: shaped profile for a matching contour — purpose = form, not general facing.
  • Up-cut vs down-cut (idea): conventional (up) vs climb (down) milling direction relative to feed — know the high-level idea and safety/setup caution as taught; don’t invent shop rules.
  • Speed & feed habit: match cutter material, workpiece, and depth — “always max spindle with zero feed” is a distractor.

Exam tip: solvent cement does not mount a milling cutter — cutters seat on arbor/spindle as taught.

4. Workholding & measuring habits

  • Machine vice: primary holding for many jobs — square, clean jaws, secure clamp.
  • Clamps & T-bolts: hold awkward shapes to the table — clamp solidly; don’t leave loose.
  • Parallels: raise and support the job square in the vice — not scrap packing randomly.
  • Vernier / micrometer: measure sizes after cuts; know least count ideas as taught — measuring ≠ cutting.
  • Setup rigidity: short overhang, solid support, correct arbor spacers — chatter and broken cutters follow floppy setups.
  • Alignment habit: indicate/check squareness before heavy cuts — “eyeball only forever” is poor practice language.

Exam tip: “Never use parallels; always pack with soft cloth only” is wrong for square support ideas.

5. Workshop safety checklist (high-yield)

  • PPE: eye protection, suitable footwear, hearing awareness where taught — no loose jewellery near rotating cutters
  • Guards: keep cutter/spindle guards fitted and effective as taught — don’t run with guards removed “for a better view”
  • Cutter handling: mount/dismount carefully; sharp edges cut skin — use correct keys/tools, never bare-hand spinning cutters
  • Chip clearing: stop spindle before clearing chips; use brush/hook as taught — never wipe moving cutters with bare hands
  • Isolation: stop and isolate before changing cutters, adjusting heavy setups, or clearing jams — prove stop
  • Clothing: no loose clothing, dangling sleeves, or long untied hair near spindle — entanglement is a classic hazard

6. Three exemplar MCQs (original)

Practice / exemplar questions — not from any leaked paper. Written for learning only.

Q1. Which statement best describes the main purpose of a milling machine arbor?
A. It only measures workpiece diameter like a micrometer
B. It mounts and drives milling cutters (especially on horizontal setups) between spindle and support
C. It permanently stores cutting oil inside the table ways
D. It replaces the need for any workholding vice on every job
Answer: B — Arbor = mounts/drives cutters on (esp.) horizontal milling.
Q2. A trainee says face milling and peripheral/slab milling are identical and always use the same cutter the same way. Which correction is best?
A. Yes — every milling cutter faces and slabs identically with no purpose difference
B. Match operation to cutter action: face milling uses face/end action for flats; peripheral/slab uses periphery teeth — don’t swap names blindly
C. Neither operation is ever done on a milling machine
D. Face milling only cools the dividing head
Answer: B — Face vs peripheral purpose differs; match cutter to job.
Q3. Which pair correctly matches a Machinist milling workshop safety habit?
A. Clear chips by hand while the cutter is still spinning at full speed
B. PPE and isolation — eye protection, guards fitted, careful cutter handling, stop before chip clearing, isolate before cutter changes, no loose clothing near spindle
C. Remove all guards permanently so you can “feel the cut better”
D. Wear dangling sleeves and jewellery to “balance” near the arbor
Answer: B — PPE, guards, stop before clearing chips, isolate for changes, no loose clothing.

7. One-week Machinist theory sprint

  • Day 1–2: Machine basics — column, knee, table, spindle, arbor; horizontal vs vertical purpose
  • Day 3: Operations — face, peripheral/slab, end milling, slotting, indexing/dividing idea
  • Day 4: Cutters — plain/slab, face mill, end mill, side-and-face, form; up/down-cut idea; speed/feed habit
  • Day 5: Workholding & measuring — vice, clamps, parallels, vernier/micrometer, rigidity
  • Day 6: Workshop safety + Employability Skills
  • Day 7: Timed Machinist mini-mock + error-notebook repair only
